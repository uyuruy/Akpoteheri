AppSheet · Google Sheets · SOPs · Quality control

Business operations & systems build

The problem

A growing subscription service needed consistent order tracking, pricing logic, quality checks and financial reporting that field staff could operate from their phones.

My contribution

I rebuilt a flat spreadsheet into a linked customer-and-order system on AppSheet and Google Sheets, with intake and quality-control flows, weighted unit calculations, status controls, a revenue logic layer and staff-facing SOPs.

What changed

A repeatable operational system supported hundreds of historical orders and replaced error-prone manual handoffs with clearer workflows and quality checks. It was designed around how staff actually work, rather than how a spreadsheet designer assumes they will work.

LifeFleet → OneBox → OneSystem

LifeBank · Government primary healthcare supply chain · Kaduna, Yobe and Kano

How the programme evolved

LifeFleet: Started with fleet management and asset utilisation for government health agencies moving products from central stores to facilities.

OneBox: Expanded into last-mile distribution and visibility across primary healthcare facilities, combining field operations and technology.

OneWallet: Addressed the financing constraint: funds were not reaching facilities effectively through the existing disbursement system. The financing workflow closed a critical gap in the delivery cycle.

OneSystem: Brought the logistics, visibility and financing layers together. LifeFleet became OneFleet within the more integrated system.

What I contributed

My role covered the operational architecture, stakeholder diagnosis, programme delivery and coordination between government stakeholders, health facilities, field teams and technology teams.

One of the most important decisions was diagnosing the programme's slow facility ordering as a funding-process problem rather than a lack of demand or user training.

Scale: The wider programme reached 470+ primary healthcare facilities and 25,000+ medicine deliveries. Figures describe the programme outcome, not an individual-only result.

What this demonstrates: Building on existing relationships, identifying structural constraints, designing solutions around real operating conditions and aligning delivery across institutions.

SmartCentre → SmartBank

LifeBank · Maternal health and blood safety · Sierra Leone deployment

The challenge

Reliable blood screening requires more than laboratory equipment. The centre needs coordinated people, technology, safe processes and a long-term operating structure so the service continues after launch.

My systems approach

SmartCentre: The physical screening laboratory.

Technology layer: The systems supporting screening processes, data and operational visibility.

Operations layer: Staffing, supply, distribution, quality processes and a viable delivery model.

My contribution

I led redesign of the initial screening-centre concept into a broader model that combined infrastructure with technology and sustainable operations. The deployment involved coordination with local institutional partners and cross-border operational logistics.

Programme reach: The Sierra Leone centre serves more than 5,000 women annually, according to the existing project record.

What this demonstrates: Launching operational infrastructure in complex environments, partnering across organisations, and designing for adoption and sustainability rather than launch day alone.
